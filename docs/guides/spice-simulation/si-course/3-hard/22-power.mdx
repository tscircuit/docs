**Status: 🚧 Not supported yet.** The pipeline uses an ideal `DDR_VDDQ`
source today. Supply impedance, decoupling and SSO coupling aren't modelled.

**Goal:** stop assuming the rails are ideal.

## The idea

When all eight DQ bits switch the same way, their current flows through the
shared VDDQ and GND paths. Any supply impedance turns that current into rail
noise, which shows up in every bit's eye and in VREF.

## Target setup

1. Extract the VDDQ plane and vias together with the decoupling capacitors,
   including their ESL and ESR.
2. Check the PDN impedance with an AC sweep (lesson 4) at the RAM's VDDQ
   balls. Target: below about VDDQ × ripple / ΔI up to the edge-rate knee.
3. Run the full byte (lesson 19), all bits switching in phase, with that PDN
   in place of the ideal source.

## Check by hand

- Target impedance: Z_target = (1.5 V × 5 %) / ΔI. With 8 bits × about 20 mA,
  that's **about 0.47 Ω**.
- The VREF divider must follow VDDQ/2 at low frequency, but be filtered
  against the fast switching noise.

## Done

At this point you have:

- the DQ and DQS eyes;
- across corners and settings;
- with coupling, real copper and real power;
- checked against the datasheet thresholds.

That's what a DDR signal-integrity signoff checks. Check every result by hand,
and treat any simulation you can't explain as unverified.
