**Status: 🟡 Runs, but don't trust the numbers yet.** The two-lane
extraction runs, but its coupling doesn't behave physically as spacing
changes. That makes this lesson about checking a model as much as about
crosstalk.

**Goal:** measure how much a switching trace disturbs its quiet neighbour,
and sanity-check the result.

## The idea

Coupled traces share mutual inductance (Lm) and mutual capacitance (Cm). When
the aggressor switches, the victim sees two kinds of noise:

- **NEXT** (near-end crosstalk) at the driver end. It scales with
  (Cm/C + Lm/L).
- **FEXT** (far-end crosstalk) at the receiver. It scales with
  (Cm/C − Lm/L) × length. In microstrip it's usually **negative**, because
  inductive coupling dominates.

Both should **fall** as the spacing grows.

## Build

The aggressor (IO1) and the victim (IO2) run straight and parallel for
20 mm. The chips' I/O pad pitch sets the spacing. The victim is driven but
held at 0:

```tsx title="15-crosstalk.circuit.tsx"
import { LineBoard } from "./teaching-board"

// Aggressor (IO1) and victim (IO2) run straight and parallel for 20 mm.
// `spacing` is centre to centre in mm; the chips' I/O pads use the same pitch.
export const Crosstalk = ({ spacing = 0.32 }: { spacing?: number }) => (
  <LineBoard name="XTALK" drive="LVCMOS33_24MA" victimSpacing={spacing} />
)

export default () => <Crosstalk spacing={0.32} />
```

`LineBoard` adds the second lane, a quiet stimulus (`bits: "0"`), and
`VIC_NEAR`/`VIC_FAR` probes when `victimSpacing` is set. The spacing
variants are `15-crosstalk-032`, `-064` and `-128`.

## What you see

24 mA aggressor, 2 ns per bit, peak victim noise:

| Spacing (centre to centre) | Lm/L | Cm/C | NEXT peak | FEXT peak |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.32 mm (gap = 1 trace width) | +0.066 | 0.145 | 71 mV | 75 mV |
| 0.64 mm (gap = 3 trace widths) | **−0.019** | 0.046 | 51 mV | **107 mV** |
| 1.28 mm (gap = 7 trace widths) | +0.017 | 0.020 | 13 mV | 27 mV |

## Check by hand, and why this fails the check

- **Cm/C falls about 3×** from 0.32 mm to 0.64 mm. That's sensible.
- **Lm/L goes negative** at 0.64 mm. Two parallel traces over a solid plane
  always have positive mutual inductance, so this is not physical.
- **FEXT grows** from 0.32 mm to 0.64 mm, the opposite of what it should
  do, before falling at 1.28 mm.
- From 0.32 mm to 1.28 mm overall, NEXT falls about 5× and FEXT about 3×.
  The end-to-end trend is right; the middle point is the warning sign.

The cause is the model, not the board. `straightened-stages` puts both
traces over one **2 mm-wide return bar**, not a whole ground plane. At
0.64 mm the traces sit near the bar's edges, and the bar's own current
crowding produces the odd mutual terms. A real plane is tens of mm wide.

This is the habit the lesson teaches: **when a trend goes the wrong way,
suspect the model before the physics.**

## What would fix it

- A return bar much wider than the trace spacing. Try
  `straightenedStageProfiles` with a custom `return.width`.
- `reduction: "routed-copper"`, which meshes the real pour, once it
  converges (see lesson 20).
