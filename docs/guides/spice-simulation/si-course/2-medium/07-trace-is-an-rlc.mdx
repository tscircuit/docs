**Status: ✅ Runs today**

**Goal:** see why a trace stops being a wire once edges get fast.

A 10 cm trace over a ground plane has roughly 300 nH/m and 100 pF/m, so about
30 nH and 10 pF in total. In this lesson you put those values in the schematic
by hand.

## Build

```tsx title="07-trace-rlc.circuit.tsx"
import { analog } from "tscircuit"

// 10 Ω driver, 1 ns edge, 10 cm trace lumped as 30 nH + 10 pF.
export default () => (
  <board routingDisabled>
    <voltagesource name="V1" waveShape="square" voltage="3.3V" frequency="10MHz"
      dutyCycle={0.5} riseTime="1ns" fallTime="1ns" />
    <resistor name="RS" resistance="10" footprint="0402" />
    <inductor name="LT" inductance="30nH" footprint="0402" />
    <capacitor name="CT" capacitance="10pF" footprint="0402" />
    <trace from=".V1 > .pin1" to=".RS > .pin1" />
    <trace from=".RS > .pin2" to=".LT > .pin1" />
    <trace from=".LT > .pin2" to=".CT > .pin1" />
    <trace from=".CT > .pin2" to=".V1 > .pin2" />
    <voltageprobe name="V_RX" connectsTo=".CT > .pin1" referenceTo=".V1 > .pin2" />
    <analog.transientsimulation name="TRACE_RING" spiceEngine="ngspice"
      duration="60ns" timePerStep="0.05ns" />
  </board>
)
```

## Run

```bash
bunx tsci simulate 07-trace-rlc.circuit.tsx
```

## What you see

At the receiver (the capacitor node), the rising edge overshoots and then
rings down:

| Peak | Time | Voltage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 3.08 ns | **3.64 V** |
| 2 | 6.53 ns | 3.49 V |
| 3 | 9.98 ns | 3.41 V |
| 4 | 13.43 ns | 3.36 V |

## Check by hand

- Ring frequency: f = 1 / (2π√(30 nH × 10 pF)) = **290 MHz**, a period of
  3.45 ns. The peaks in the sim are 3.45 ns apart ✓
- Damping: ζ = (R/2)·√(C/L) = 5 × √(10p/30n) = **0.091**. Each peak's
  overshoot is about 0.56× the one before, and e^(−2πζ) ≈ 0.56 ✓

## Try this

Make the driver slower (`riseTime="5ns"`). Once the edge is slower than the
ring period, the overshoot almost disappears. **The edge rate sets whether a
trace matters, not the clock frequency.**
