---
title: <assembly.explosion.move />
description: Move named assembly parts or all members of another explosion stage.
---

`assembly.explosion.move` describes one straight translation inside an [`assembly.explosion.stage`](./assembly-explosion-stage.mdx). See that page for a complete runnable example and playback controls.

```tsx
<assembly.explosion.stage name="lift-cover">
  <assembly.explosion.move
    targetRefs={["cover", "remove-bolts"]}
    offZ="12mm"
  />
</assembly.explosion.stage>
<assembly.explosion.stage name="remove-bolts" prevStageRef="lift-cover">
  <assembly.explosion.move targetRefs="bolt1, bolt2" offZ="10mm" />
</assembly.explosion.stage>
```

## Move properties

| Property | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| `targetRefs` | An array of names or a comma-separated string selecting parts, subassemblies, or other explosion stages. |
| `targetRef` | Singular alternative to `targetRefs`. Supply exactly one of them. |
| `offX`, `offY`, `offZ` | Translation along each axis. Numbers are millimetres; unit strings such as `"12mm"` are also accepted. Omitted axes are zero. |

References resolve relative to the stage's owning assembly. A qualified path can address a nested part, such as `cover.window`. Blank, missing, ambiguous, or cyclic targets are errors.

## Whole-stage movement

In the example, `remove-bolts` identifies all parts targeted by that stage. They move with the cover by 12 mm before their own stage adds another 10 mm. Targeting a stage preserves earlier displacement and does not replay its moves. Use `prevStageRef` or `nextStageRef` on the stage to control timing.

A part included directly and through a subassembly or stage moves only once per instruction. Separate instructions accumulate, including when they target the same part.

## Axes and sequential moves

Offsets are directions in the owning assembly's resolved local frame, measured in millimetres. An unrotated assembly uses right-handed circuit coordinates: +X right, +Y top, +Z above the PCB. Mounting can rotate those local axes. Core resolves each direction into a world-space vector before writing Circuit JSON.

The axes supplied on one move act simultaneously. Two moves execute sequentially: an `offZ` move followed by an `offX` move makes a lift-then-slide path. During partial playback, each move receives an equal portion of its stage's progress.

Selected subassemblies move all descendant CAD and reference-surface centers. Surface axes do not rotate. Movement of a target containing PCB or cable geometry is unsupported, and no collision clearance is verified.
