## Overview

A `<subcircuit />` is a powerful organizational element in tscircuit that
represents a collection of elements that are tightly coupled. Subcircuits are
often used for a small functional block, such as a voltage regulator.

Within a subcircuit, you can have a custom autorouter or isolated nets from the
larger circuits. You can also re-use reference designators. Your subcircuit is
essentially isolated from other subcircuits.

## Exposed ports on a standalone schematic

When a subcircuit is the root of a single-sheet schematic, tscircuit draws a
boundary around its contents and connects its public ports to the internal
circuit. Export the subcircuit directly, without wrapping it in a `<board />`.
This is available in `tscircuit@0.0.3187` and later.

Use `exposedNets` to select the internal nets that form the public interface.
This example adapts the real
[CC2340R5 reference design](https://tscircuit.com/ShiboSoftwareDev/cc2340r5-reference-design):
all 27 components remain, including the RF network, crystals, decoupling, reset,
and debug header. Eight previously unused GPIOs are connected to named nets;
together with power, ground, reset, and SWD, they form 13 exported terminals.

Open `cc2340r5.tsx` in the file picker to see the internal circuit and exported
net names. `schPinArrangement` sets the sides and ordering of the terminals;
core assigns their positions after laying out the contents and routes wires to
the internal pins. Optional `schWidth` and `schHeight` set minimum boundary dimensions.

You can instead declare [direct `<port />` children](./port.mdx#expanded-subcircuit-ports)
with `connectsTo` to map public names to internal pin selectors. `exposeNets`
exposes all internal nets; use `exposedNets` when only selected signals should
be public.

The automatic boundary applies to a standalone expanded subcircuit with public
ports. [Nested subcircuits and ordinary groups](./group.mdx#expanded-subcircuit-boundaries)
do not automatically get this frame. `showAsSchematicBox` still displays a
collapsed symbol instead of the internal circuit.

## Reuse Reference Designators

Reusing reference designators is typically considered a bad practice, but in
tscircuit reference designators are intelligently prefixed prior to being
written on the silkscreen. This means that you can design your subcircuits
without worrying about whether or not a reference designator has been previously
used.

Within a subcircuit, you'll never select inside of another subcircuit without
explicitly specifying the subcircuit `name` in a selector. This means you never
need to worry about other `R1` or `C1` selectors from other subcircuits, they
will not be selected unless you explicitly include a subcircuit selector
e.g. `.somesubcircuit .R1`

## Configuring the Autorouter

Subcircuits can have a custom autorouter configuration. This will be inherited
by any children subcircuits.

To specify a custom autorouter configuration, just set the `autorouter` property
on a `<subcircuit />` element.

```tsx
<subcircuit autorouter="auto">
  <resistor name="R1" resistance="1k" footprint="0402" />
  <resistor name="R2" resistance="1k" footprint="0402" />
  {/* ... */}
</subcircuit>
```

Specifying custom autorouter settings for subcircuits can be extremely useful
when you have a tricky section of components that have special requirements.

Read more about [the autorouter prop here](./board.mdx#setting-the-autorouter).
