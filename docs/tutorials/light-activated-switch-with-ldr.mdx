## Overview

A **light-activated switch** (or dark-activated night light) is a classic analog sensing circuit that automatically switches a load—such as an LED, buzzer, or relay—on or off based on ambient light levels. By combining a **Light Dependent Resistor (LDR)** with a fixed resistor (or potentiometer) in a voltage divider and feeding the output into the base of an **NPN bipolar junction transistor (BJT)**, you can build a reliable threshold switch without requiring a microcontroller.

In this tutorial, you will learn how the LDR voltage divider works, calculate component values for reliable switching around the transistor's `0.7V` base-emitter threshold, and design both schematic and PCB layouts in **tscircuit**.

## Components Needed

| Reference | Component | Typical Value / Part | Role in Circuit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `J1` | 2-Pin Header | `2.54mm` pitch (`pinrow2`) | Connects `5V`–`9V` DC supply (`VCC` and `GND`) |
| `R_LDR` | Light Dependent Resistor (LDR) | GL5528 (`~1kΩ` bright light to `>100kΩ` dark) | Varies resistance inversely with ambient light intensity |
| `R_BIAS` | Fixed Bias Resistor (or Potentiometer) | `10kΩ` – `50kΩ` (`0805` or `pinrow3` trimpot) | Forms a voltage divider with `R_LDR` to set the light threshold |
| `R_BASE` | Base Current-Limiting Resistor | `1kΩ` (`0805`) | Limits maximum base current into `Q1` when `R_LDR` is high |
| `Q1` | NPN Bipolar Junction Transistor | 2N2222, BC547, or SMD `MMBT2222A` (`sot23`) | Acts as a low-side electronic switch for the load |
| `R_LED` | LED Series Resistor | `330Ω` – `470Ω` (`0805`) | Limits current through the indicator LED to safe levels (`~15mA`) |
| `D1` | Indicator LED | Red, Yellow, or White LED (`0805`) | Illuminates when the transistor switch turns on |

## How the Circuit Works

### 1. The LDR and Voltage Divider

A **Light Dependent Resistor (LDR)**, or photoresistor, is made of a high-resistance semiconductor such as cadmium sulfide (CdS). When photons strike the photosensitive surface, bound electrons jump into the conduction band, dramatically lowering the component's resistance:

- **In bright light:** `R_LDR` drops to a few hundred ohms or `1kΩ–2kΩ`.
- **In darkness:** `R_LDR` rises to `100kΩ–1MΩ`.

To convert this resistance change into a control voltage, we pair `R_LDR` with a fixed resistor `R_BIAS` across the supply rails (`VCC` and `GND`). Using the standard voltage divider equation, the voltage at the midpoint (`V_DIV`) when `R_BIAS` is connected to `VCC` and `R_LDR` is connected to `GND` is:

```text
V_DIV = V_CC * (R_LDR / (R_BIAS + R_LDR))
```

### 2. NPN Transistor Threshold (`V_BE ≈ 0.7V`)

An NPN transistor (`Q1`) remains **OFF** (cut-off region) as long as its base-to-emitter voltage (`V_BE`) stays below approximately `0.6V–0.7V`. Once `V_DIV` exceeds `0.7V`, current flows into the base terminal, driving `Q1` into saturation so current can flow from its **collector** to its **emitter** and illuminate the LED (`D1`).

Let's verify both operating states with `V_CC = 9V` and `R_BIAS = 10kΩ` (Dark-Activated Night Light configuration):

1. **Bright Environment (`R_LDR ≈ 500Ω`):**
   ```text
   V_DIV = 9V * (500 / (10000 + 500)) ≈ 0.43V
   ```
   Because `0.43V < 0.7V`, transistor `Q1` stays **OFF** and the LED remains **OFF**.

2. **Dark Environment (`R_LDR ≈ 100kΩ`):**
   ```text
   V_DIV = 9V * (100000 / (10000 + 100000)) ≈ 8.18V (clamped to ~0.7V at Q1 base)
   ```
   Because the open-circuit divider voltage is well above `0.7V`, base current flows through `R_BIAS` and `R_BASE` into `Q1`, saturating the transistor and turning the LED **ON**.

## Step-by-Step Circuit Design in tscircuit

### Step 1: Build the LDR Voltage Divider

We start by placing a 2-pin power header (`J1`), the upper pull-up bias resistor (`R_BIAS`), and the lower photoresistor (`R_LDR`) connected between `net.VCC`, `net.VDIV`, and `net.GND`. Because an LDR is a two-terminal resistive sensor, we use a `<resistor />` with a through-hole axial footprint (`axial_p5.08mm`) so a standard 5mm photoresistor can be soldered directly onto the board.

## Circuit Modifications & Enhancements

### 1. Reversing Operation (Bright-Activated Switch)

To make the LED turn **ON in bright light** and **OFF in darkness** (for example, a solar tracker wakeup trigger or laser tripwire receiver), simply swap the positions of `R_LDR` and `R_BIAS` in the voltage divider:

- Connect `R_LDR` between `VCC` and `net.VDIV`.
- Connect `R_BIAS` (`10kΩ`) between `net.VDIV` and `GND`.

Now, when bright light lowers `R_LDR` to `~1kΩ`, `V_DIV` rises to `9V * (10k / 11k) ≈ 8.18V`, saturating `Q1` and turning on the load.

### 2. Adding Hysteresis (Preventing Twilight Flicker)

When ambient light changes slowly at dusk or dawn, a single transistor stage enters its linear active region and may cause the LED to dim gradually or flicker due to shadow noise. You can eliminate this by feeding `net.VDIV` into an op-amp comparator (such as an LM393) or Schmitt trigger IC (74HC14) with positive feedback before driving `Q1`.

### 3. Driving Heavy Loads with a Relay

To switch high-power AC lamps or 12V DC strips, replace `D1` and `R_LED` with a 5V/9V relay coil between `VCC` and `.Q1 > .collector`. Always place a **flyback diode** (such as a `1N4148` or `1N4007`) in reverse parallel across the relay coil (`cathode` to `VCC`, `anode` to `.Q1 > .collector`) to clamp inductive voltage spikes when the transistor turns off.

## Practical Applications

- **Automatic Night Lights & Garden Lamps:** Automatically illuminate pathways and solar garden LEDs after sunset.
- **Street Light Controllers:** Switch outdoor lighting grids on at dusk and off at daybreak.
- **Optical Beam-Break Alarms:** Pair a laser diode pointed at `R_LDR` to trigger an alarm buzzer whenever an object interrupts the beam.
- **Camera Exposure & Day/Night Mode Triggers:** Switch security cameras between IR night-vision LEDs and daytime color filters.
