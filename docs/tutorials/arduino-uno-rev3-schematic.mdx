## Overview

The Arduino Uno is the most classic beginner board, and its schematic is a
great exercise in reading a real-world two-chip design. In this tutorial we
recreate the **Arduino Uno Rev3 schematic** in tscircuit, one subsystem at a
time:

- **Power**: barrel jack into an NCP1117 5V LDO, a LP2985-33 3.3V LDO, and a
  P-FET + comparator circuit that picks between USB power and the barrel jack
- **USB interface**: a USB-B connector wired through an ATmega16U2 USB-serial
  bridge, with a 500 mA resettable fuse and 22 ohm series termination
- **MCU core**: the ATmega328P with its 16 MHz crystal, decoupling, AREF cap
  and reset circuit (10k pull-up, pushbutton, and the famous auto-reset cap)
- **Board extras**: the buffered D13 "L" LED, the power/digital/analog headers
  and the ICSP header

This tutorial is schematic-only. For reference, compare your result against the
official Arduino UNO Rev3 schematic linked from the
[Uno Rev3 product page](https://docs.arduino.cc/hardware/uno-rev3).

## Power Input and Regulation

When powered from the barrel jack (7-12 V), the NCP1117-5.0 LDO produces the
5V rail, with an input decoupling cap and a 47uF bulk cap on its output. The
LP2985-33 then derives the 3.3V rail from 5V for the ICSP header and 3.3V pin.

## USB Power Path and Source Selection

When USB is plugged in, VBUS first passes through a 500 mA resettable fuse
(`F1`) and then reaches the 5V rail through the P-channel MOSFET `T1`. One half
of the LMV358 acts as a comparator: it watches the barrel-jack voltage against
the 5V rail and drives the FET gate, so the board automatically runs from the
barrel jack whenever both sources are present. The USB data lines leave the
connector through 22 ohm series resistors toward the USB-serial chip.

## USB-Serial Bridge: ATmega16U2

The ATmega16U2 runs from the 5V rail (VCC) and senses USB power on UVCC. Its
own 16 MHz crystal keeps the USB clock stable, and `C_UCAP` decouples the 3.3V
output of its internal regulator. On the firmware side:

- `TXD`/`RXD` are crossed into the ATmega328P UART
- `DTR` pulses through a 100 nF cap (`C_AUTO`) into the 328P RESET net - this
  is the auto-reset that makes serial uploads "just work"
- `HWB` is held low through 10k so the chip boots its USB firmware normally
- its own RESET is pulled up to USBVCC and routed to the 16U2 ICSP header

## The ATmega328P Core

The main MCU is the ATmega328P in a 28-pin DIP on the Rev3 board. Around it:

- a 16 MHz crystal with two 22 pF load capacitors on XTAL1/XTAL2
- 100 nF decoupling on VCC and AVCC, plus a 100 nF cap on AREF
- a 10k pull-up and a pushbutton on the RESET net, plus the auto-reset cap
  arriving from the 16U2's DTR line
- the crossed UART on PD0/PD1, and the GPIO nets that fan out to the headers
- PB3/PB4/PB5 double as MOSI/MISO/SCK for the ICSP header

## Status LED, Headers and the Full Schematic

Two finishing touches, straight from the reference design:

- The "L" LED on D13 is driven through the **second half of the LMV358 wired
  as a unity-gain buffer**, so the LED's load never disturbs the SPI clock on
  D13. The buffered output goes through a 1k resistor into the orange LED.
- The expansion headers mirror the real board: the 8-pin power header (NC,
  IOREF, RESET, 3V3, 5V, GND, GND, VIN), the D0-D7 header, the D8-D13 header
  with GND/AREF/SDA/SCL, the analog header (A0-A5 plus power taps) and the
  6-pin ICSP header.
