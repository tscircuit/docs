## Overview

The **ESP32-WROOM-32** is an industry-standard Wi-Fi and Bluetooth module powered by the dual-core Tensilica Xtensa LX6 microprocessor. Creating an ESP32 schematic requires careful attention to power rails, strapping pin states, and the auto-programming circuit needed for seamless flashing from the Arduino IDE or ESP-IDF.

This tutorial guides you through building a complete, production-ready ESP32 module circuit in tscircuit:

1. **ESP32-WROOM-32 Pinout & Bank Mapping**: Power, antenna keepout, RF matching, and strapping pins.
2. **Stable 3.3V High-Current Power Rail**: LDO selection and bulk filtering to survive 500mA Wi-Fi current bursts.
3. **Automatic Bootloader & Reset Circuit**: Dual-transistor (`DTR`/`RTS`) logic for automated USB flashing.
4. **Boot/Reset Pushbuttons**: Manual hardware overrides with debouncing capacitors.
5. **Breakout Headers**: Clean access to I2C, SPI, ADC, and UART peripherals.

## Critical Engineering Guidelines

### 1. Power Supply Sizing & Wi-Fi Current Spikes
The ESP32 module can draw momentary current spikes exceeding **450–500 mA** during Wi-Fi calibration and RF transmission.
- Never power the module from a 100mA or 150mA regulator.
- The `AP2112K-3.3` provides up to **600 mA** with ultra-low dropout (250mV at 600mA).
- Place a **22 µF bulk ceramic capacitor (C3)** directly adjacent to the ESP32 `3V3` pin to absorb transient dips and prevent brownout resets (`Brownout detector was triggered`).

### 2. The EN Pin Power-On Timing Requirement
Espressif hardware guidelines mandate that the `3V3` power rail must reach at least **2.3V** before the `EN` (chip enable) pin rises above **1.6V**.
- An RC delay circuit with a **10 kΩ pullup (R1)** and **1 µF capacitor (C5)** guarantees this monotonic rise delay.
- Omitting the 1 µF capacitor on `EN` is the #1 cause of cold-boot crashes on custom ESP32 boards.

### 3. Automatic Download Logic Table
The cross-coupled transistor pair (`Q1` & `Q2`) allows USB-UART converters (`CH340`, `CP2102`, `FT232`) to toggle `EN` and `IO0` into ROM bootloader mode without manual button pressing:

| DTR State | RTS State | Q1 State | Q2 State | EN Pin | IO0 Pin | Mode |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| High (1) | High (1) | OFF | OFF | High | High | Normal Execution |
| Low (0) | High (1) | OFF | ON | High | **Low (0)** | Bootloader Ready |
| High (1) | Low (0) | ON | OFF | **Low (0)** | High | Chip Reset |
| Low (0) | Low (0) | OFF | OFF | High | High | Interlock (No Reset) |

### 4. Strapping Pins to Keep Safe
- **IO0**: Must be pulled High during normal boot; pulled Low only during flashing.
- **IO2**: Must be floating or pulled Low during flashing; has an internal pulldown.
- **IO12**: Controls internal flash voltage (MTDI). Must be kept Low to avoid locking flash to 1.8V on standard 3.3V modules.
- **IO15**: Controls boot message silencing. Defaults to High.
