## Overview

This tutorial extends the **ESP32 Reference Schematic** (`tscircuit/docs-old#39`) into a complete, manufacturable **PCB layout and routing workflow** in **tscircuit** (`tscircuit/docs-old#44`).

When laying out a mixed-signal wireless microcontroller like the **ESP32-WROOM-32E**, physical placement and copper routing directly affect Wi-Fi/BLE antenna range, power-rail stability during `2.4 GHz` TX bursts (`~500 mA` peaks), and USB-UART signal integrity.

In this tutorial, you will learn how to:

1. **Configure a 4-layer PCB stackup** (`top`, `inner1` GND plane, `inner2` `3.3V` power plane, `bottom`) with JLCPCB-compatible design rules.
2. **Place the ESP32-WROOM-32E module** at the top board edge so the PCB antenna overhangs or sits over a copper-free keepout region.
3. **Group and place decoupling capacitors (`100nF` + `22uF`)** within `1–2 mm` of the `3V3` (`pin2`) and `EN` (`pin3`) pins.
4. **Place the USB-C receptacle (`J1`), LDO regulator (`U2` AMS1117-3.3), and USB-UART bridge (`U3` CP2102N)** along the bottom edge to minimize high-speed USB stub lengths.
5. **Add ground & power copper pours (`<copperpour />`)** to ensure low-impedance return paths.

## Step-by-Step PCB Layout Rules for ESP32

### 1. Antenna Placement & Keepout Zone

Per the [Espressif Hardware Design Guidelines](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-hardware-design-guidelines/en/latest/esp32/pcb-layout-design.html):

- **Edge Placement:** Position the ESP32 module (`U1`) at the top edge of the board (`pcbY={11}`) so the PCB trace antenna sits at the perimeter.
- **No Copper Under Antenna:** Never place `<copperpour />`, signal traces, or mounting holes directly underneath the antenna region on **any layer** (`top`, `inner1`, `inner2`, `bottom`).
- **Clearance:** Maintain at least `15 mm` of clearance from tall metal components (such as electrolytic capacitors or shielding cans) around the antenna zone.

### 2. Power Supply & Decoupling Floorplan

The ESP32 radio draws rapid current spikes (`>450 mA`) during Wi-Fi packet transmission:

| Component | Value | Footprint | Placement Rule |
|-----------|-------|-----------|----------------|
| `C_RF` | `100nF` | `0402` | Within `1.5 mm` of `U1.V3_3` (`pin2`) to shunt `2.4 GHz` switching noise |
| `C_BULK` | `22uF` | `0805` | Right beside `C_RF` on the `3.3V` feed to supply burst current |
| `R_EN` + `C_EN` | `10kΩ` + `100nF` | `0402` | Placed adjacent to `U1.EN` (`pin3`) to prevent false resets from EMI |
| `U2` (`AMS1117-3.3`) | `800mA` LDO | `sot223` | Placed between the bottom `5V` entry and `U1`, with `C_IN` (`10uF`) and `C_OUT` (`22uF`) close to its pins |

### 3. Layer Stackup & Return Paths

Using a 4-layer board (`layers={4}`) significantly improves RF performance and simplifies autorouting:

- **Layer 1 (`top`):** High-speed UART (`TXD0`/`RXD0`), crystal/RF decoupling, and SMD component pads.
- **Layer 2 (`inner1`):** Unbroken solid `GND` plane (`<copperpour layer="inner1" connectsTo="net.GND" />`) providing a continuous reference plane underneath all top-layer traces.
- **Layer 3 (`inner2`):** Dedicated `3.3V` power plane (`<copperpour layer="inner2" connectsTo="net.V3_3" />`).
- **Layer 4 (`bottom`):** Secondary GPIO breakouts leading to `J_LEFT` and `J_RIGHT` `2.54mm` headers.

### 4. UART & Strapping Pin Routing Guidelines

- **Crossed UART Traces:** Route `U3.TXD` → `U1.RXD0` and `U3.RXD` → `U1.TXD0` with short, direct traces avoiding parallel runs near the RF antenna.
- **Strapping Pins (`IO0`, `IO2`, `IO12`, `IO15`):** Keep pull-up/pull-down resistors (`R_BOOT` on `IO0`) close to the module pins to minimize capacitive loading during boot-mode sampling.
