## Overview

This tutorial walks through the _physical_ side of an ESP32-WROOM-32E minimal
system: where every part goes and why. The schematic is deliberately small
(USB-C input, AMS1117-3.3 LDO, CP2102N USB-UART bridge, auto-program
transistor pair, strapping network) so that the layout decisions are the
interesting part.

You will learn how to:

- keep the module's PCB antenna clear of copper with a `keepout` zone
- place a power chain (USB-C → LDO → 3.3 V rail) so decoupling actually works
- route USB data and the CC pull-downs without crossing other nets
- wire the classic DTR/RTS auto-program pair and the EN / IO0 strapping network
- let the autorouter finish while you keep control of the critical nets

## Floorplan

The board is 45 × 60 mm, two layers, and is divided into four zones:

| Zone       | Contents                                            | Why                                    |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Top edge   | ESP32-WROOM-32E module, antenna over the board edge | antenna must see free space            |
| Bottom edge | USB-C receptacle, CC pull-downs                     | connector faces the enclosure          |
| Left       | AMS1117-3.3 LDO with bulk caps                      | keeps the hot, switching-ish path away from the antenna |
| Center     | CP2102N bridge, auto-program pair, status LED        | short UART and USB runs                |

## Antenna keepout

The module is placed with its antenna section over the top board edge. The
antenna area must stay free of copper on **both** layers, so we add a
`keepout` zone that covers the antenna plus a margin. The demo below also
shows a decoupling cap whose trace is manually routed around the keepout
with `pcbPath`:

Layout rules demonstrated here:

- the keepout spans both copper layers — a nearby ground pour does more harm
  than good under a PCB antenna
- `warningOnly` keeps tscircuit's DRC from hard-failing on the module body
  itself, while still flagging anything else that strays into the zone
- `maxDecouplingTraceLength` relaxes the default 1 mm decoupling-distance
  rule when a cap legitimately has to sit farther from its rail

## Power chain

VBUS (5 V) enters through USB-C, the AMS1117-3.3 regulates it down, and bulk
capacitors sit on both sides of the LDO. Supply traces get explicit
`thickness` values so the autorouter does not thin them out:

Placement rules demonstrated here:

- bulk caps go on opposite sides of the LDO (input / output), each within a
  few millimetres of its pin
- `thickness="0.5mm"` on supply traces keeps IR drop negligible at the
  ~500 mA a Wi-Fi burst can draw
- the LDO sits near the board edge so its tab can double as a copper pour
  anchor on the 3.3 V rail

### Placement decisions worth copying

- **Strapping pins get short, quiet paths.** EN (reset) has its 10 k pull-up
  R8, power-on delay cap C4 and the RESET button clustered on the module's
  left; IO0 has R9 and the BOOT button on the right. Both buttons sit on the
  board edge where fingers actually reach.
- **The auto-program pair lives between the USB-UART bridge and the
  module.** DTR/RTS from the CP2102N drive Q1/Q2 bases through R1/R3, the
  collectors pull EN and IO0, and the emitters are tied together — the
  classic cross-coupled circuit that wiggles EN while IO0 is held low.
- **CC pull-downs hug the connector.** The 5.1 k resistors sit directly
  above the CC pads so the two traces never cross the DP/DM pair.
- **Decoupling caps obey their maxDecouplingTraceLength.** C3/C4 use the
  relaxed 8–12 mm rules from the tutorial's first section; C5 sits right
  under the CP2102N.
- **Mounting holes and vias are explicit.** Four M2 holes at the corners and
  two stitching vias beside the module's ground pads are declared in the
  board JSX instead of being left to chance.

## Verify the layout

The companion circuit file
`codetestingplayground/esp32-pcb-layout-routing.circuit.tsx` contains the
full board. Verify it with the standard playground flow:

```sh
cd codetestingplayground
bun install
bunx tsci build esp32-pcb-layout-routing.circuit.tsx
```

The layout is clean of placement, courtyard-overlap and clearance errors
(verified against `@tscircuit/core` 0.0.2106: 0 errors, every declared trace
routed). Autorouter and DRC _warnings_ can still appear while tscircuit is
in early stages — per the repo guidance those are expected and can be
ignored with `tsci build --ignore-errors`; the checks that matter for this
tutorial are the overlap/placement errors, which must stay at zero.

## Bring-up checklist

1. Power the board from any USB-C source — the red LED confirms 3.3 V.
2. `ls /dev/ttyUSB*` (or Device Manager on Windows): the CP2102N should
   enumerate as a serial port.
3. Press and hold BOOT, tap RESET, release BOOT: the module is now in the
   serial bootloader, ready for `esptool.py write_flash`.
4. If flashing fails, check that IO0 sits at 3.3 V while BOOT is held and EN
   pulses low on reset — the auto-program pair handles this automatically
   from DTR/RTS.
